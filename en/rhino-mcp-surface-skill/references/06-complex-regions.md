# Complex regions

## Branches and junction centers

Establish branch axes, sections, and transition width. Compare A: analytic/swept branches with bridge bands and compatible central four-sided patches; B: a larger reconstructed section/network domain; C: SubD concept followed by the representation required for delivery.

Pairwise BlendSrf solves two sides, not an entire three-way center. Patch can initialize a shape, followed by boundary reconstruction, but its name is not G2 evidence. Star reflections and pinching call for more area, different boundaries, or a relocated junction.

## Multi-sided corners

First consider restoring a parent and trimming. Otherwise compare local four-sided patch groups with a wider layout change. Check corner derivative compatibility. If locked neighboring constraints conflict, report that and enlarge the reconstruction. Do not mask real gaps with JoinEdge/RebuildEdges.

## Fading and variable-width transitions

Define initial height/strength, disappearance position, width, and silhouette change. Control decay with sections. Avoid collapsing rails to a pole in a critical glossy region; extend a wider parent and trim when useful. Variable-radius fillets still need end-reflection checks.

For twisted blends inspect pairing/direction/seams, section correspondence, width change, bulge/Same height shapes, then corners. Extract useful sections from different trial blends and reconstruct with Sweep2 if needed. Real face-edge continuity constraints must be revalidated.

## Saddles and alternating curvature

Negative Gaussian curvature may be intended. Control both section families. Extra waves suggest conflicting sections, order, or parameterization before missing points. Mean curvature may hide cancellation; inspect principal/Gaussian curvature and sections.

## Openings, recesses, handle pockets

Trim simple openings in good parents and construct walls independently. Soft recesses need a base and sufficient transition width; deep ones may require wider relayout. Check sections through the deepest point, depth/width ratio, and offset intersections.

## Panel gaps and flanges

Fair exterior parents precede derived panels, gaps, and flanges. Keep visible gap width, flange depth, and material thickness separate. Glass can be planar, single-curved, or double-curved according to evidence. Pipe-cut bands followed by Blend/Sweep2 are options, not fixed-radius or constant-surface-width guarantees.

## Reconstruction and engineering branches

Register scans, establish units, identify analytic geometry/symmetry/noise, then reconstruct fair sections and parents. Report deviation distributions, maxima, quantiles, outliers, and functional datums rather than fitting all noise.

ShrinkWrap approximates a wrapping mesh. QuadRemesh/SubD/ToNURBS is an approximation route, not guaranteed dimension control or design layout. Editable reverse engineering often requires intent-based refitting. MeshToNURB may create one face per polygon and excessive complexity.

For sheet development check Gaussian curvature/flattening strain and process constraints; visual resemblance to bent sheet does not establish developability. For molding define pull direction, parting, and material; do not apply generic shrinkage automatically.
